Bolt Bench · storm controller

Pot Wiring Card

Three B10K linear pots and a chaos button into an ESP32 DevKit.

3.3 V — never 5 V

The ADC pins are not 5 V tolerant, and the strip's 5 V rail will be right there on the same bench. Feeding a pot from it kills the pin, often the chip. Use a different wire colour for the two rails.

One pot

Knobs

PinKnobRange
GPIO32Palette5 detents, one whole look each — storm, tornado, voldemort, halloween, red planet
GPIO33Speed11 detents, 0.25× to 4× geometric; detent 5 is exactly 1.00×
GPIO34Level11 detents in 10% steps — brightness for now, storm severity later

All three knobs are detented, holding position until you move three quarters of a step. A continuous knob on a noisy 12-bit ADC is the worst of both worlds — never quite still, never repeatable. Each pot is read once every 750 ms, averaging 32 conversions.

The palette knob carries a whole look per detent, not a hue. Rotating hue alone leaves saturation wherever the export left it, and at low saturation every hue is the same near-white — so the knob appears dead. Fine hue control lives in the bench; the knob is for choosing.

All three are ADC1. ADC2 stops working entirely once WiFi is enabled — that rules out GPIO 0, 2, 4, 12–15 and 25–27. GPIO34 is input-only, which is a feature here: it cannot be driven as an output by mistake.

Chaos button

A momentary pushbutton between GPIO27 and ground. The internal pull-up does the rest, so it is two wires and no resistor — and it shares the ground rail already running to the pots, making it one extra conductor in the loom.

Do not put it on GPIO34–39. Those pins have no internal pull-ups and would need an external one. Any ordinary output-capable pin works: 13, 14, 25, 26, 27, 33.

A tap fires every root channel at once, staggered 90 ms apart so it reads as a cascade rather than one white frame, then holds the storm at maximum for 2.5 s and eases back to your knob positions over another 2 s. Tapping again restarts it. The dashboard shows severity: CHAOS while it runs.

Six wires, not eleven

  • 3.3 V — daisy-chain to all three pin 3 1 wire
  • Ground — daisy-chain to all three pin 1 1 wire
  • Hue wiper GPIO32
  • Speed wiper GPIO33
  • Level wiper GPIO34
  • Chaos button — other leg to the ground rail GPIO27

Chain the two rails pot-to-pot inside the box so only five conductors leave it. Zip-tie a strain relief where the loom exits — panel pots have an anti-rotation tab, so either drill a 2 mm hole for it or snip it off, otherwise the pot spins in the panel and shears the wires.

Test before writing any code

void setup() { Serial.begin(115200); }
void loop() {
  Serial.printf("%5d %5d %5d\n",
    analogReadMilliVolts(GPIO_NUM_32),
    analogReadMilliVolts(GPIO_NUM_33),
    analogReadMilliVolts(GPIO_NUM_34));
  delay(100);
}

Expect a smooth sweep from roughly 0 to 3300 mV, with about ±10 mV of jitter sitting still. Use analogReadMilliVolts rather than analogRead — it applies the chip's factory calibration, and the raw reading flattens near both rails so the last of the knob travel does nothing.
